Against the fifty themes

Templates aren’t the problem. Pretending a template is a brand is.

There is nothing wrong with a good theme. It is a sensible starting point: tested, accessible and quick to launch. The trouble starts when the theme becomes the brand, when the same hero, the same product grid and the same reviews carousel appear on a furniture dealer, a skincare label and a bike shop.

Customers notice, even if they can’t name it. A storefront that looks like every other storefront asks to be compared on price, because there is nothing else to compare.

What we change first

We rarely start by redesigning everything. We start with the three moments that carry the most weight: the first screen, the product page and the moment of adding to the bag. Those are the places where a brand either sounds like itself or like a template.

Typography, photography and pacing do most of the work. A different grid does surprisingly little on its own.
