One object, one price, one action

The quietest product pages often sell the hardest. Notes on merchandising with restraint.

A good shop assistant doesn’t list every product in the store when you walk in. They show you one thing, tell you why it matters and let you decide.

Online, we tend to forget this. Pages fill up with badges, countdowns, cross-sells and five ways to pay before anyone has looked at the product properly.

Give the object room

When we design a product page, we start with the object on its own: large, well lit and uninterrupted. Then we add only what helps someone decide: the price, the one detail that matters most and a single clear action. Everything else moves further down, where people who want it will find it.

Restraint isn’t minimalism for its own sake. It is a way of telling the customer that the product is worth their full attention.
